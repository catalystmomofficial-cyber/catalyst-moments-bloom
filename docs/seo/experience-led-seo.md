# Experience-Led SEO — Catalyst Mom content strategy

**Standing rule for every session. Applies to blog posts, YouTube scripts,
landing copy, and any long-form content.**

Source: strategy from Harry Sanders (@harrysandersseo), verified against
Google's own guidance before adopting (see "Why this holds up" below).

---

## The one idea

Google ranks content that is **unique, specific, and authentic.** In 2026, a
real **first-hand story is a ranking factor** — not a nice-to-have. Generic,
assemble-anywhere content is being actively demoted; lived experience is the
one signal AI-generated content cannot fake.

For Catalyst Mom this is a moat, not a chore: a brand built by a couple who
*lived* the TTC → pregnancy → postpartum journey has real stories, real
numbers, and real recovery that no competitor and no AI can copy.

## Why this holds up in 2026 (verified, not taken on faith)

- **E-E-A-T** — Google added the extra "E" for **Experience** in Dec 2022.
  First-hand experience is now an explicit quality signal.
- **Helpful Content system** — folded into Google's **core ranking as a
  continuous signal in March 2024.** "Unique, specific, authentic" is exactly
  Google's "people-first content."
- **AI-spam demotion** — Google is actively demoting mass-produced AI content.
  The differentiator that survives is genuine experience.

This is the **written-content sibling** of the rules already in this project:
the `information-gain-prompt` mandate and "never fabricate — real experience
comes from the user." Experience-led SEO is those rules, sharpened for search.

## The 4-step method (run this on every piece before delivering)

1. **Reject the generic title.** If a competitor could publish the same
   headline, kill it. "Diastasis Recti Exercises" is generic. Do not ship it.
2. **Rewrite around an experience only Catalyst Mom has.** Re-anchor the angle
   on the couple's story, a real member journey, or a specific process detail
   nobody else can tell.
3. **Anchor it in a real number or a real story.** One concrete stat, timeline,
   or first-hand moment beats ten adjectives. (A real number the brand can
   stand behind — never an invented one.)
4. **Flag what needs first-hand input.** If the strongest version needs a
   detail only the founder knows, *ask for it* — list the 2–3 questions only
   they can answer. Do not fill the gap with fabrication.

## Worked example (the method, not a template)

- ❌ Generic: **"5 Diastasis Recti Exercises That Work"**
  (any site can write this; nothing to rank on)
- ✅ Experience-led: **"The DR self-check I did on my own kitchen floor —
  and the 4-week rebuild that actually closed the gap"**
  - Anchored in a *specific scene* (kitchen floor, self-check) and a *real
    outcome* the brand can substantiate.
  - Questions to the founder before writing: *What did the self-check feel
    like? What week did you first notice change? What's the honest before/after?*
  - Every stat used must be one the founder confirms is real. Where a number
    is claimed, it must be verifiable — otherwise cut it.

## Guardrails (non-negotiable, inherited from the project mandate)

- **Never fabricate stories, stats, testimonials, or outcomes.** Real
  experience must come from the user. If it isn't confirmed, it doesn't ship.
- **Medical honesty.** No guaranteed medical outcomes ("close your DR," "reduce
  tearing" as a promise). Describe the process and the real experience; keep
  claims defensible. Add provider-clearance framing where relevant.
- **Voice.** Warm, plain language, evidence-based, for moms 25–40 across TTC,
  pregnancy, and postpartum.
